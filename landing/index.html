RFC 2119
Key words for use in RFCs to Indicate Requirement Levels
Scott Bradner · Harvard University · March 1997 · 3 pages

            
Standards were already full of words like MUST and SHOULD. No one had said exactly what they meant.

            
These three pages did. More than 4,400 RFCs have pointed back to them since.
References recorded by the IETF Datatracker, October 2026.

          

          

            
Ten words, one meaning each.
Any RFC can borrow them by opening with this sentence.

            
MUST: no exceptions.
Skip one, and you haven’t built the standard.

            
MUST NOT: never.
An absolute prohibition.

            
SHOULD: yes, unless there’s a real reason not to.
And you need to understand what skipping it costs.

            
SHOULD NOT: no, unless there’s a real reason to.
Sometimes it’s acceptable, even useful. Weigh it first.

            
MAY: truly optional.
With it or without it, products still have to work together.

            
Capitals matter.
In 2017, RFC 8174 made it official: only the uppercase words carry these meanings.

          

          
From here on, when an RFC says MUST, it means exactly this.
