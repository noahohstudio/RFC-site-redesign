Glossary

The words you’ll meet in the archive, in plain language.

RFC
“Request for Comments” — a numbered document in the RFC Series. Once published, an RFC is never changed; a new RFC is written instead.
Internet-Draft
A working document. Drafts that are approved are published as RFCs; the rest expire.
Status
What kind of document it is: Proposed, Draft or Internet Standard on the standards track, or Best Current Practice, Informational, Experimental or Historic.
STD
The Internet Standard sub-series: a stable number for a mature standard, pointing to the RFCs that currently make it up (STD 97 is RFC 9110).
BCP
Best Current Practice: advice on how to run the internet and its processes (BCP 14 is RFC 2119 and RFC 8174).
FYI
“For Your Information”: a retired sub-series of introductory documents.
Obsoleted
Replaced: a newer RFC takes over completely. The older RFC stays in the archive, exactly as it was.
Updated
A newer RFC changes or adds to part of this one. Read the two together.
Errata
Corrections reported after publication. They are listed beside an RFC, never written into it.
Stream
Who published it: the IETF, IAB, IRTF, Independent Submissions or the Editorial stream. Early RFCs, from before streams existed, are marked Legacy.
MUST, SHOULD, MAY
Requirement key words, defined in RFC 2119 and RFC 8174 (together, BCP 14).
ABNF
Augmented Backus–Naur Form: the grammar notation many RFCs use to define syntax (RFC 5234).
